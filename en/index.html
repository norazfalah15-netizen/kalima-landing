Understand the whole world, not just America and Britain

More than a billion people speak English, and most of them aren’t native speakers. So with English you can talk with people from many different countries, and understand sources and videos from all over the world.

English is the most widely used language in the world: the language of the internet, study, work, and travel. Most of the content you need is written in it first, and translated late — or never.

And when you master it, doors open on their own: jobs that weren’t open to you, clients from outside your country, scholarships and study abroad, and unlimited learning resources. And every word you learn gives you more than its meaning: confidence you speak without hesitation, and independence you reach the source directly with no middleman.

And the best part is that it grows with you: every level you reach opens wider content and more people, and what you learn today stays with you for life.

My story
Why I built the “Kalima” app

For many years I tried to learn English, and failed every time. I’d start excited, memorize random word lists, and after two weeks I’d forget everything and go back to zero. The problem wasn’t enthusiasm — it was that I studied without a plan.

Until I heard about the most important 5000 words chosen by Oxford University, which cover about 95% of everyday conversation. I decided to start with them, and right away I noticed the difference: the words I memorized, I actually ran into, because they were chosen carefully, not randomly.

But I noticed that memorizing alone isn’t enough. I needed something to make the word stick and help me understand it more deeply — and that was reading. So I started reading graded books, one after another. The difference was clear, but each book on its own costs about 12 dollars, and the whole set adds up to a lot of money.

So I decided to make the books myself: 165 graded books from beginner to advanced, all built into the course — and the books are just one part of it, not the whole course.

And within three months of studying this way, I went from a very beginner level — I could barely form a sentence — to reading books and understanding them without stopping at every line, and speaking in sentences of my own. The difference wasn’t intelligence or more time, just because the path was clear and I didn’t waste my time on randomness.

That’s where the idea came from: why not put everything in one place? All the words, and all the graded books from A1 to C1, instead of paying for each book separately.

And I want to be honest with you: Kalima isn’t a magic wand. It’s a course that gathers for you the right resources and an organized path to master the language — but the result depends on your own effort and study.

If you walk in and browse without commitment, nothing will change. But if you follow the order and study regularly, you’ll see a real difference — just like it happened for me.
